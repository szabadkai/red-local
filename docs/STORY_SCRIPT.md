# Red Riding Hood campaign script

This proposed story campaign follows the five clearings in the browser combat prototype: Lantern Wood, Broken Crossing, Moss Cavern, Ruined Chapel, and Thorn House. A delivery to Grandmother becomes a rescue journey through a forest whose people are losing their voices. Each stage introduces a character, changes Red's understanding of the journey, and ends with a distinct boss encounter.

The tone is a dark woodland fairy tale with warmth, dry humor, and frightening silhouettes. Preserve the basket, the warning about the path, the charming stranger, the Huntsman, and the grandmother disguise. Let Red recognize the deception and drive the rescue. Target 60–90 minutes for a first playthrough, with roughly six to eight minutes of optional dialogue and staged scenes across the campaign. All encounters and story systems below are proposed additions.

## Story premise

Grandmother tends the lanterns that keep the forest roads safe. Her weekly letter has arrived without a single word on it. Mother sends Red with bread, medicine, and Grandmother's old deck of cards, asking her to find out what happened before nightfall.

The Wolf has learned to steal the voices of those he swallows. He uses familiar voices to lure people off the road. Before Red's journey, he intercepts Grandmother's warning letter and eats the words themselves, leaving its envelope to be delivered. He has also silenced the chapel bell, whose sound forces stolen voices out of hiding. While Red crosses the forest, he reaches Grandmother's cottage first.

Red's cloak and cards carry Grandmother's protective magic. Dark Red is that same magic burning fiercely; using it does not corrupt Red or change the ending. Rowan initially mistakes that magic for the Wolf's influence.

The emotional thread is simple: Red begins by following instructions because she wants to prove she can manage alone. She finishes by making her own decisions and accepting help without surrendering responsibility.

## Principal characters

| Character | Voice and role | Payoff |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Red | Practical, impatient, observant. Gets quieter when frightened. | Recognizes the false Grandmother and leads the escape from the Wolf. |
| Mother | Warm, busy, worried; gives useful advice rather than prophecies. | Meets Red and Grandmother on the homeward road. |
| Grandmother | A former keeper of the forest roads. Affectionate, blunt, still capable. | Helps Red fight from inside the Wolf; admits she should have asked for help sooner. |
| The Wolf | Courteous as the stranger; intimate and cruel when using stolen voices. | Loses his borrowed voices until only an ordinary animal's snarl remains. |
| Rowan the Huntsman | Tired, economical with words, embarrassed by failure. | Becomes an ally after a duel; holds the way home open during the finale. |
| Pip the goblin ferryman | A self-appointed toll collector with more nerve than strength. | Supplies the cave route, then returns with his boat for the ending. |
| Sister Wren | The chapel's bell keeper, imprisoned inside its corrupted guardian. | Restores the bell and identifies the Wolf's weakness. |

## Campaign progression

| Stage | Immediate objective | Boss | Story turn |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Lantern Wood | Follow Grandmother's lantern trail. | The Briar Hart | The forest's protectors are being wounded and twisted. |
| 2. Broken Crossing | Reach the far bank after the bridge collapses. | The Tollkeeper | The Wolf can borrow familiar voices; Grandmother's lantern goes out. |
| 3. Moss Cavern | Take Pip's underground route to the chapel. | Rowan the Huntsman | An apparent enemy becomes an ally; restore the bell before approaching the cottage. |
| 4. Ruined Chapel | Free the bell and ring it. | The Bell Widow | The Wolf has swallowed Grandmother; ringing the bell reveals that she is alive. |
| 5. Thorn House | Enter the cottage and rescue Grandmother. | The Wolf | Red sees through the disguise, fights inside the Wolf, and brings Grandmother home. |

## Prologue at the village gate

**Cutscene P01 — The empty letter. About 40 seconds.**

Close on a blank letter beside a loaf of bread. Its envelope bears Grandmother's familiar handwriting. Mother wraps a medicine bottle in a cloth; Red reaches for the basket before she has finished.

**MOTHER:** Your grandmother has never sent me a short letter. This is a first.

**RED:** Perhaps she finally ran out of complaints.

Mother puts the old deck into Red's hand. Red makes a card vanish badly. Mother retrieves it from her sleeve.

**MOTHER:** Take these. She taught you for a reason.

**RED:** To beat her at cards?

**MOTHER:** Nobody beats her at cards. Home before dark. Stay by the lanterns. If you hear someone calling from the trees, find their face before you follow.

Mother tucks the bread into the basket.

**MOTHER:** And don't leave the crusts.

**RED:** I know. “The crust is what holds the bread together.”

Red imitates Grandmother's gravelly voice. Mother laughs, then holds the basket a moment longer than necessary before letting go.

Control begins at the gate. Objective: **Bring the basket to Grandmother.**

## Stage 1 Lantern Wood

The opening is warm: gold light, wind in leaves, distant birds. Teach movement on the village edge, sword attacks against an isolated scout, then card use in a clearing. Beyond it, lanterns lean toward the trees as if something has pulled them. The birds gradually fall silent.

Keep the three existing ember pickups as pieces of fallen trail lanterns. Their placement guides exploration; collecting them rekindles the exit lantern after the boss. They are ordinary route objectives, not permanent upgrades.

**Cutscene S1A — The helpful stranger. About 30 seconds.**

A well-dressed traveler stands beside a signpost, cleaning mud from his gloves. His shadow has pointed ears. Red notices the sign points away from the lanterns.

**STRANGER:** Taking supper somewhere?

**RED:** Medicine. The bread is for complaints.

**STRANGER:** The old woman beyond the chapel, then. Her garden is lovely this time of year.

He offers a white flower. Red leaves it in his hand.

**RED:** You know her?

**STRANGER:** Everyone in these woods knows your grandmother.

He gestures toward a shorter path. A crash shakes the marked road ahead. Red heads toward the sound instead; he watches her go, still holding the flower.

**Boss — The Briar Hart.** A forest guardian is trapped in thorn growth, with an iron wolf trap clamped around one antler. It blocks the only intact crossing over a ravine.

- First phase: the Hart scrapes the ground, lowers its antlers, then commits to a straight charge. Dodge past it and attack during recovery.
- Second phase: at half health, the thorns root it in place. Clearly marked root eruptions travel across the floor; safe gaps remain reachable by ordinary jumps.
- Victory breaks the growth and trap. The Hart survives. It lowers its antlers to create a short bridge, turning the defeated boss into the route forward.

**Cutscene S1B — A wounded guardian. About 15 seconds.**

Red picks a strand of pale fur from the trap's hinge. The Hart touches its nose to her basket, then retreats.

**RED:** Sorry. That loaf's spoken for.

At the exit lantern, the stranger's discarded flower is already wilting. No explanation is spoken.

## Stage 2 Broken Crossing

The river has flooded a small mill settlement. Houses remain inhabited, but nobody answers from the windows. Introduce moving platforms, waterwheel timing, and enemies on separated ledges. A figure on the opposite bank appears to wave with Mother's silhouette, then slips out of view.

**Cutscene S2A — A very reasonable toll. About 25 seconds.**

Pip stands on an overturned bucket beside a tiny ferry. A sign reads “SAFE CROSSING.” Beneath it, in smaller letters: “MOSTLY.”

**PIP:** Toll's one loaf.

**RED:** For that boat?

**PIP:** You're paying for the captain.

From the mill comes Mother's voice: “Red? This way.” Red takes one step, then stops.

**RED:** She doesn't know I'm here.

Pip quietly turns his sign facedown.

**PIP:** It used my brother's voice yesterday.

The waterwheel starts turning against the current. Something in the mill hauls a chain taut.

**Boss — The Tollkeeper.** A hulking mill guard, wrapped in chains and driven by the Wolf's curse, has seized the ferry mechanism. Pip calls him “the other toll collector.”

- First phase: a raised hook signals a horizontal chain sweep; a two-handed lift signals a ground slam. The distinction teaches jumping versus repositioning.
- Second phase: the Tollkeeper anchors his chain to the wheel, briefly rotating two arena platforms before slamming. Fixed bank ledges remain safe waiting positions.
- Recovery exposes the lock on his chest. Sword hits and any damaging card hand can break it. No particular poker hand is required.
- Victory shatters the cursed lock and frees the exhausted guard. His normal voice returns; he has no memory of the fight.

**Cutscene S2B — The last light. About 25 seconds.**

Pip ferries Red across. On the distant hill, one warm window goes dark. Show the cottage curtain pulled shut by a long hand; do not show Grandmother's fate yet.

**PIP:** That was the old keeper's light.

**RED:** Row faster.

**PIP:** I am rowing faster.

At the far bank he points beneath the broken bridge.

**PIP:** The upper road's gone. Old quarry tunnel comes out by the chapel. Find Rowan. Big axe. Terrible manners.

Red breaks off a small piece of bread and leaves it on the boat.

**RED:** For the captain.

Objective changes to **Reach the chapel through Moss Cavern.**

## Stage 3 Moss Cavern

Roots pierce the cavern roof. Old hunter camps and abandoned lanterns show Rowan has been searching here for days. Use vertical routes, falling stones with visible dust warnings, and ranged enemies. Safe pockets let the player read short messages carved into posts: “DO NOT ANSWER” and “IT KNOWS YOUR NAME.”

Let the player see Rowan cutting down a wolf-shaped shadow across an unreachable gap before meeting him. Establish competence and fear before the misunderstanding.

**Cutscene S3A — The wrong shadow. About 30 seconds.**

At the tunnel mouth, Rowan plants his axe across the path. Red's cloak casts a dark, moving silhouette in the lantern light, regardless of her current gameplay form.

**ROWAN:** Put down the basket. Step away from the cloak.

**RED:** You first. From the axe.

From behind Red, her own voice says: “Too late, Huntsman.” Her mouth has not moved. Rowan raises his weapon.

**RED:** That wasn't me.

**ROWAN:** It never is.

**Boss — Rowan the Huntsman.** A defensive duel with a frightened human, rather than another large monster. His meter is labeled **Resolve**. Defeat disarms him; it never kills him.

- First phase: Rowan plants his feet before a heavy axe cut. Attacking into his guard is ineffective; bait the swing and punish the recovery.
- Second phase: he throws the axe along a clearly indicated line, then pulls it back on a rope. The return path stays visible, and he is vulnerable while retrieving it.
- Ordinary movement and sword attacks must suffice. Cards can exploit openings; Dark Red gives its normal advantages and does not trigger a punitive special response.
- On defeat, the axe lodges in a root. A shadow lunges at Rowan from behind. Red cuts it down in a short staged action.

**Cutscene S3B — A useful apology. About 40 seconds.**

**ROWAN:** You could have left me to it.

**RED:** I still need directions.

He notices the stitched edge of her hood.

**ROWAN:** Your grandmother made that. She used to mend mine.

**RED:** Then help me find her.

Rowan points to the chapel above them.

**ROWAN:** That bell can shake loose the voices he's taken. He sent something up there to stop it ringing. I've been trying to get through.

**RED:** We'll try from this side.

Rowan lifts a fallen beam to expose the chapel stair. Red passes beneath it. He stays to brace the damaged passage and bring the stranded mill villagers through afterward.

## Stage 4 Ruined Chapel

The chapel's pews are covered in pale silk. Bell ropes twitch although the air is still. Introduce rising traversal through balconies and a short sequence in which the player cuts silk anchors to open the belfry. Each anchor releases a fragment of a captive voice.

**Cutscene S4A — The bell keeper. About 25 seconds.**

Sister Wren is visible inside a suspended cocoon beneath the bell. She beats against it in a steady rhythm. Red copies the rhythm against a railing with her sword hilt. Wren points upward.

**RED:** You want it rung?

Wren nods. Behind her, what appeared to be the bell's wooden support unfolds into long jointed legs.

**Boss — The Bell Widow.** A creature of chapel timber, silk, and stolen clothing, built around the captive keeper. Its visual resemblance to a veiled mourner should become clear only when it moves.

- First phase: the Widow plucks a rope, marking where debris will fall. Between attacks, it lowers a leg onto a reachable balcony; striking it breaks the silk armor.
- Second phase: it crosses the bell and sends sound rings along the floor. Jump the rings, use side ledges to evade descending limbs, and attack during its landing recovery.
- Damage to the boss never harms Wren. At zero health, it collapses, exposing the final binding. A normal sword strike releases the bell; no timed input sequence is needed.

**Cutscene S4B — A voice from the house. About 40 seconds.**

The bell rings once. Silk falls through the chapel like snow. Wren catches the railing as Red helps her down.

Across the forest, familiar voices spill out of empty windows. From the distant cottage comes Grandmother's voice, faint but unmistakable.

**GRANDMOTHER:** Red? If that's you, mind the back step. It's rotten.

Red laughs once, involuntarily, with relief.

**RED:** She's alive.

**WREN:** Inside him. The bell made him loose his grip. Only for a moment.

Wren points to the cottage; lanterns along its path are lighting again.

**WREN:** I'll keep ringing. Bring her out.

End on Red already moving. Objective: **Find Grandmother inside Thorn House.**

## Stage 5 Thorn House

Start outside a small, recognizable cottage. The garden is dense with white flowers identical to the stranger's. Inside, ordinary domestic rooms stretch into impossible corridors. Reuse familiar platforming patterns in distorted form: shelves become ledges, roots break through floors, and a pantry doorway opens into darkness deeper than the house.

Rowan reaches the garden after guiding the villagers to safety. He cuts an escape route through the outer thorns while Red enters. His help makes the eventual escape possible; Red handles the confrontation.

The final three embers light the cottage's hearth lantern and unlock the bedroom corridor. Place the last checkpoint here, before the disguise scene.

**Cutscene S5A — Grandmother in bed. About 50 seconds.**

A figure lies under Grandmother's quilt. Her spectacles sit crookedly on a long nose. The camera stays at Red's eye level. The player should recognize the Wolf before he admits it.

**WOLF AS GRANDMOTHER:** Come closer, little one. Let me see you.

Red sets the basket on a chair, just outside his reach.

**RED:** What big ears you have.

**WOLF:** All the better to hear you coming.

**RED:** And what big hands.

**WOLF:** All the better to hold you.

Red takes the loaf out of the basket.

**RED:** Shall I cut off the crusts?

**WOLF:** Yes, dear. I never liked them.

She sets the bread down and draws her sword.

**RED:** Where is she?

The figure sits up. The spectacles fall. His voice passes through Mother, Rowan, and Grandmother before settling into the stranger's soft courtesy.

**WOLF:** You came such a long way. Surely you knew what was at the end.

**Boss — The Wolf, keeper of stolen voices.** One finale with three connected sections.

**First section: the bedroom.** The Wolf throws aside the quilt and tears open the walls. He fights with long lunges, claw sweeps, and leaps between the bedroom floor and exposed rafters. His breath draws inward before a bite; this is a visual and audio tell. Stolen voices accompany attacks but never replace their readable animations. At half health, he staggers into the hearth shadow.

**Cutscene S5B — Swallowed. About 15 seconds.**

Grandmother's hand reaches from the shadow. Red grabs it; the surrounding room folds into an enormous mouth. The swallowing is a transition after a successful phase, never an unavoidable attack that removes health.

**WOLF:** Everything that comes into my woods ends up with me.

Red keeps hold of the hand as the light goes out.

**Second section: inside the Wolf.** A short playable space made of rooms he has swallowed: a mill doorway, a chapel arch, Grandmother's kitchen. Use cloth, smoke, and silhouettes rather than anatomical gore. Still holding her hand, Red lands beside Grandmother, whose legs and torso are pinned to her chair by three thorn knots.

**GRANDMOTHER:** Did you bring the medicine?

**RED:** You could have mentioned the wolf in your letter.

**GRANDMOTHER:** I tried. He ate the words.

The bell sounds through the walls at regular intervals, illuminating safe platforms and revealing the knots. Break them while avoiding sweeping shadows. Ordinary sword strikes always work; card attacks provide faster alternatives. Released voices briefly form lights that point toward the next safe landing.

Once free, Grandmother pulls a long red stitch from her cloak's hem and anchors it through a crack of daylight. Red follows the thread upward and cuts open the shadow seam. Grandmother climbs after her.

**Final section: the garden.** They emerge through the Wolf's torn shadow beneath the cottage wall. Rowan braces the thorn gate; Grandmother takes shelter beside him. Restore Red's health and save a retry checkpoint before returning control.

The Wolf has lost his voices and can only snarl. Use a shorter final health bar and faster variations of the lunge and sweep already learned. Between patterns, the chapel bell makes his shadow separate briefly from his body, exposing a broad damage window. These windows repeat indefinitely, so survival never depends on a lucky hand or a single opportunity.

On the final hit, the stolen shadows stream toward the bell. The Wolf shrinks to his true size and collapses into ash and pale fur. The thorns loosen. The cottage remains damaged but real.

**Cutscene S5C — Breakfast. About 35 seconds.**

Red sits on the broken step, shaking now that the danger has passed. Grandmother sits beside her, takes the medicine, and makes a face.

**GRANDMOTHER:** Still tastes like ditch water.

**RED:** Mother says to finish it.

Grandmother finishes it. A pause.

**GRANDMOTHER:** I thought I could keep the road safe by myself.

**RED:** Runs in the family.

Rowan retrieves the basket from the wreckage. Red unwraps the crushed loaf.

**RED:** The bread's ruined.

Grandmother breaks it into pieces and hands one to each of them.

**GRANDMOTHER:** Tastes like bread.

## Epilogue on the homeward road

**Cutscene E01 — Room for one more. About 30 seconds.**

Dawn. Pip waits at the river with an extra plank lashed across his boat. Wren rings the chapel bell once. The freed mill guard repairs the crossing; the Hart watches from the trees.

Mother meets them at the village lantern. She embraces Red first, then Grandmother.

**MOTHER:** I said before dark.

**RED:** We brought breakfast.

Mother sees the little piece of bread remaining. She divides it anyway.

Final playable moment: walk through the village gate with Grandmother beside Red. Fade after crossing the threshold. The closing image is the basket on the kitchen table, Grandmother's cards beside it, and four mismatched cups.

End title: **The road is open.**

## Cutscene and encounter production plan

Stage conversations should use in-engine sprites, simple camera moves, and portrait dialogue. Reserve illustrated stills, if desired, for the empty letter, the false Grandmother, and the final kitchen table. The swallowed world can reuse existing scenery in impossible arrangements.

| Sequence | Trigger | Control and replay behavior |
| --- | --- | --- |
| P01 | New story journey | Skippable; skipping starts at the gate with the delivery objective. |
| S1A, S2A, S3A, S4A | First arrival at the marked encounter | Activate only after nearby combat ends; pause simulation for dialogue. |
| S1B, S2B, S3B, S4B | Corresponding boss defeated | Save the victory before playback; skipping performs the same world changes. |
| S5A | Enter bedroom corridor | Save before playback; retries offer a short boss introduction instead. |
| S5B | Bedroom phase completed | Save phase completion; skip transitions directly into the swallowed rooms. |
| S5C | Wolf defeated | Save campaign completion, then play the garden scene. |
| E01 | Garden scene ends or is skipped | Skippable; retain the final walk and completion screen. |

Give every boss an entrance checkpoint and a repeatable retry with full health and a fresh normal hand. The finale additionally checkpoints the swallowed rooms and the garden. Retry restores the corresponding phase state, including Grandmother's position and released bindings. Keep story state separate from ordinary combat state so a reload cannot replay a victory reward or leave an exit locked.

Every boss must be beatable with movement, the sword, and whatever hand the normal deck supplies. Pairs and stronger combinations shorten openings; rare hands and Dark Red are bonuses. During boss recovery, allow a safe opportunity to collect earned card choices. Do not require gambling minigames, new deck upgrades, or combat against Grandmother.

Dialogue has manual advance, readable speaker labels, captions for offscreen voices, and a hold-to-skip option. Never disguise an actual control prompt or change player inputs to simulate the Wolf's deception. Pause the world while dialogue or card choice UI is open. After a scene, return the camera to Red and clear held attack inputs before resuming play.

New character art is needed for Mother, Grandmother, Pip, Rowan, Wren, and the Wolf's stranger and disguise appearances. The Hart and Bell Widow need distinct silhouettes and animations; the Tollkeeper can build on the guard archetype. Existing forest, cavern, chapel, cottage, card, and Red assets provide the environmental and player foundation. New boss behaviors, dialogue playback, persistent story flags, and phase checkpoints require implementation.

For the first playable story slice, build the prologue, Lantern Wood, the stranger meeting, and the Hart encounter together. That slice should establish whether short conversations, combat pacing, and a boss that changes the environment work before expanding the remaining stages.
